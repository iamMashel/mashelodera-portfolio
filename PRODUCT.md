# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences land on this site, often in the same 15 seconds:

- **Hiring managers / recruiters** at AI, SaaS, healthtech, and fintech companies screening for a frontend engineer who also has product-design judgment. They skim, they want proof fast, they care about shipped outcomes and whether the person can own a surface end to end.
- **Founders / product leads** looking for a freelancer or contractor to design and build a marketing site, SaaS dashboard, or AI product prototype. They want to see relevant work, understand how Mashel works, and reach out with low friction.

Context: mostly desktop during work hours, but a meaningful share arrives on mobile from LinkedIn, X, or a referral link. They give the page seconds before deciding to scroll or leave.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio that converts a skim into a contact. It exists to prove, not to claim: that Mashel Odera designs *and* ships production frontend for AI-era products. Success = a qualified visitor reaches out (or downloads the resume) because the work and the way it's presented made the decision easy. Secondary: rank and read well when shared, and give recruiters a fast, credible yes.

## Brand Personality

Precise, candid, quietly confident. The voice of an engineer who designs: specific nouns and verbs, real numbers, no hype. Three words: **precise, modern, grounded.** It should feel like a senior practitioner showing their work, not a junior decorating a template. Emotional goal for the visitor: trust and a small jolt of "this person is good."

## Anti-references

- The generic dark dev-portfolio template (terminal-green-on-black, particle backgrounds, "Hi, I'm X 👋" + tech-logo soup). The current Lovable site drifts toward the safe end of this.
- AI-scaffold tells: numbered service sections (01/02/03), tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every heading, the hero-metric template (big number + tiny label rows as the whole proof), identical icon-heading-text card grids, Discover→Design→Build→Test→Ship generic process timelines.
- Marketing buzzword voice (streamline, empower, supercharge, seamless, world-class, cutting-edge).
- AI-cream / warm-beige "editorial restraint" body backgrounds. This brand is bright white + one saturated accent, not parchment.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't claim.** Every assertion ("I build production React") is backed by a case study, a metric, or a live link. Proof beats adjectives.
2. **Outcome before output.** Lead each project with what changed (problem solved, result), not the tech stack. The stack is a footnote, not the headline.
3. **One signature, held consistently.** Bright white, near-black ink, one magenta accent, oversized confident type. The restraint IS the design; the accent is used with intent, not sprinkled.
4. **Practice what you preach.** This site is itself a portfolio piece: fast, accessible, responsive, motion that's purposeful. If it's sloppy, the claim collapses.
5. **Low-friction trust.** Make the next step (read a case study, see the code, get in touch) obvious and one click away at all times.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified against the white canvas (muted grays are the risk; keep them dark enough).
- Full keyboard operability, visible focus-visible states, semantic landmarks and headings, accessible names on all controls and links.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation (crossfade/instant fallback). Motion never gates content visibility.
- Responsive and legible from 320px up; touch targets ≥44px; measure capped ~65–75ch for prose.
